# Script Structure Reference

The default structure for every video. Use this alongside `sub-skills/script.md` for the full pipeline workflow, or standalone with `youtube-script-creator` for quick script creation.

## Problem-first structure (default)

Most YouTube scripts about new products take the "here's what X just dropped and here's what it means" angle. Mits's channel takes a different approach. Lead with the problem first, then show how it's solved.

Why this matters: for beginner audiences especially, leading with the problem creates relevance before introducing the product. The viewer understands why they should care before they're told what to care about.

### Structure pattern
1. Here's a problem you can relate to
2. Here's why it was hard before
3. Here's what changed
4. Here's proof, watch me do it
5. Here's what it costs
6. Here's what to do next

## Standard chapter map (10 to 12 minute video)

Use these timestamps as a starting point. Adjust based on demo complexity.

| Chapter | Start | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0:00 | Frame the problem in a way the viewer feels personally |
| Intro and promise | 0:30 | What you'll learn, examples previewed, the "why this is different" |
| The problem | 1:30 | Concrete scenarios, pain points, proof point from a real company if possible |
| What changed | 3:30 | The core solution explained in plain English, transition to demo |
| Platform orientation | 4:00 | Quick tour of the interface before any clicks |
| First demo | 4:45 | Walk through the primary use case |
| Deeper capability | 6:00 | Second layer of what the tool does |
| Behind the scenes | 7:30 | What's happening that the viewer can't see |
| Extended demo or second example | 8:45 | Reinforce with a different angle |
| Pricing | 9:30 | Framed as surprisingly affordable, not as a sales pitch |
| Outro | 10:15 | Restate the core shift, CTA, next video tease |

## Writing each section

### Hook (0:00 to 0:30)

Goal: make the viewer feel the problem before you introduce the product.

Template:
1. Start with something the viewer has thought about or experienced.
2. Name the hidden complexity that makes it hard.
3. List the specific things that make it hard (3 to 4 items, delivered fast).
4. Land the promise: "Anthropic just changed that. And in this video, I'll show you exactly what you can build."

Avoid:
- Starting with "In this video..."
- Starting with a rhetorical question that the viewer has to guess at.
- Mentioning the product name in the first 10 seconds.

### Intro and promise (0:30 to 1:30)

Goal: tell the viewer exactly what they're going to get and why it's worth staying for.

Template:
1. "If you... this video is for you." (Define the audience in their own words.)
2. "By the end of the next twelve minutes..." (List three specific things they'll walk away with.)
3. State what you're NOT doing, to set expectations. "I'm not going to bore you with the agent answering questions..."
4. End with "Let's go straight in."

### The problem (1:30 to 3:30)

Goal: make the viewer understand the cost of the problem before you solve it.

Use two practical examples where possible, from different business contexts (for example, one support-focused, one sales-focused). This helps different viewers see themselves in the video.

Include a proof point: a real company that solved this, with specific numbers (for example, "Rakuten's team built four agents in one week instead of months").

### What changed (3:30 to 4:00)

Goal: explain the solution in one paragraph of plain English. No jargon. No feature lists.

Template:
"What [company] built is [plain description]. They took [the hard stuff] and they handle it for you. You focus on [the thing you actually care about]. They handle everything else."

Then transition: "And the proof isn't in some benchmark. It's something you can actually see. Let me show you."

### Platform orientation (4:00 to 4:45)

Goal: orient the viewer before you start clicking around.

Walk through the main sections of the interface in one pass. Do not demo anything yet. Just point and name.

"Here's what you see: [section one]. [Section two]. [Section three]. That's the lay of the land."

### Demo sections (the biggest risk area)

Demo sections have the highest risk of dead air. Every loading, processing, or connecting moment must have pre-written filler narration.

#### The three-phase demo structure

Every demo follows three phases (see `jack-roberts-techniques.md` for full detail):

**Phase A: Setup (before the action)**
Before typing a single line of code or prompt, explain what you're about to do and why it matters. Never just start clicking.

**Phase B: Void-filler (during the action)**
AI takes time to generate. Use this loading time for: explaining the underlying strategy, dropping quick hacks, building community connection, contrasting with how it used to be done, or setting up what comes next.

**Phase C: Review (after the action)**
Once the AI finishes, immediately review the output on screen. React positively ("how good is that") but also act as a critic.

### Behind the scenes section (after the demo)

Goal: show the viewer something they couldn't easily see from just watching the demo.

This is often the audit trail, the session log, the vault, or anything that shows the viewer "this is deployable in real environments because you can see what's happening."

### Pricing (9:30 to 10:15)

Goal: make the pricing feel like a relief, not a sales pitch.

Template:
1. Lead with the number, no preamble.
2. Explain what's actually charged.
3. Contrast with what most enterprise AI tools cost.
4. Give the smallest possible entry point ("£X in credit to start").

### Outro (10:15 to end)

Goal: restate the core shift, tell them what to do next, tease the next video.

Template:
1. "So here's what changed: [the core shift, one sentence]."
2. "[The specific thing from this video] used to take [old cost]. Now it's [new cost]."
3. "If you want to try it, [URL] is in the description."
4. "Subscribe if this was useful. I break down AI tools and infrastructure every week for people actually building things."
5. "Next up, I'm covering [next video topic]. [One sentence tease]."

## Mid-video CTA

Drop the mid-video CTA at around the 3:00 mark, right before the first demo chapter begins. Keep it tied to the content, don't interrupt the flow.

Template:
"Actually, quick pause. If you're finding this useful, hit subscribe. I break down AI tools every week for people who want to actually use them. More like this in your feed."

## Retention re-hook

At around the 60 percent mark (roughly 7:00 in a 12 minute video), drop a retention re-hook right before the next chapter:

Template:
"Now here's the part most videos on this topic skip over, and honestly it's the reason this is actually useful in real business environments..."

This re-engages viewers who are thinking about clicking away.
