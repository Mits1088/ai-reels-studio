# Creator Voice Profile: Mits

This is the definitive voice profile for all YouTube scripts. Apply every rule here. Load at Phase Y1 before writing any script, and before any standalone `youtube-script-creator` session.

---

## Who Mits is

Mits runs a GoHighLevel agency helping physiotherapy clinics, medical spas, and wellness businesses in the UK. He tests AI tools on real business problems every week. His YouTube channel is aimed at beginners curious about AI, not developers already building with APIs.

---

## Confirmed catchphrases (use these)

- **"Let's go straight in"** — the consistent opener for every video. Use it at the end of the intro section, right before the first chapter begins.
- **"How good is that"** — reaction to AI output or a completed demo step. Use when something works well on screen.
- **"Check this out"** — draws attention to something about to happen on screen.
- **"That's amazing"** — positive reaction, used sparingly so it keeps its impact.
- **"Mate"** — occasional casual address, UK English. Use at most once per video for warmth.

---

## Rejected phrases (never use)

- "Dude"
- "Bro"
- "Freaking cool"
- "Grab that coffee and let's dive in" (this is a different creator's catchphrase)

---

## Credibility anchor rules

- Use occasionally, not every video.
- When used, reference the GHL agency experience: "My name is Mits, I run a GHL agency helping wellness businesses scale, and I test AI tools on real business problems every week."
- Skip the anchor when the video topic doesn't connect to business automation or client work.
- Keep it to one sentence. Do not labour the point.

## Language patterns (always follow)

### Register
- Conversational, direct, personal
- Heavy use of "you" — make it about the viewer, not abstract
- Never formal, academic, or corporate in tone
- Comfortable with rhetorical questions

### Simpler verb forms
- "what one does" not "what one is"
- "what it actually cost" not "what it actually costs"
- Prefer present simple over perfect tenses.

### Direct, compact phrasing
- "way more affordable than you think" not "way more affordable than you'd expect"
- "it covers everything you need" not "it covers everything you need to decide if this is something you want to try"
- Drop filler words. If a sentence works without a word, remove it.

### Heavy use of "you"
- Make every point personal.
- Avoid abstract phrasing like "teams" or "organisations." Prefer "you" or "your team."
- "Most people" not "most teams" when generalising.

### Casual contractions
- "aren't sure" / "it's" / "that's" / "here's" / "you're"
- Never use stiff or formal constructions ("it is," "do not," "cannot" in prose).

### Natural sentence starters
- "Let me show you..."
- "Here's the thing..."
- "Here's what I mean..."
- "Imagine you..."
- "Here's what happens..."

### Spoken rhythm over grammatical polish
- "By the end of this video, in the next twelve minutes" reads weirdly but sounds right spoken.
- Trust the ear. If it sounds right out loud, it is right.

### Active phrasing
- "Anthropic just removed all of that" not "Anthropic has eliminated the infrastructure layer."
- Subject, verb, object. In that order. Short sentences.

### Scenario framing
- "Imagine you have..."
- "Imagine you've just closed a deal..."
- "Let's say you want to..."
- Put the viewer in the situation before explaining the solution.
- Frame concepts by what they replace, not by defining them abstractly.

### Short punchy sentences between longer ones
- Use three-word sentences to punctuate longer explanations.
- "Simple idea." / "That's the real shift." / "Done." / "One week instead of months."

## Words Mits does NOT use

Flag and replace these if they appear in drafts:

- "Observability" → use "you can see exactly what the agent did and why"
- "Scaffolding" → use "the infrastructure around it" or "the plumbing"
- "Harness" → use "the runtime" or "the environment it runs in"
- "Leverage" (as verb) → use "use"
- "Utilise" → use "use"
- "In order to" → use "to"
- "At this point in time" → use "now"
- "Going forward" → drop entirely

**Per-topic vocabulary check:** During Phase Y0 (demo-ingest), note which technical terms the creator uses naturally in their spoken notes. Those terms are in-scope. Terms they avoid or paraphrase around are out of scope for the script. Do not carry specific replacements from one video into another unless the same avoidance pattern appears in that video's demo-ingest notes.

---

## Metaphor preferences

Use metaphors that are:
- **Business-focused** (hiring an employee, having a digital assistant)
- **Everyday life-based** (librarian fetching a book, an assistant taking notes)
- **Sports or fitness-based** (warming up, training, drills)

Avoid metaphors that are:
- Sci-fi or superhero-based ("Iron Man suit")
- Dramatic or overwrought
- Too clever for the audience

---

## Spoken delivery rules

- Read every sentence out loud before finalising.
- If the sentence makes you stumble, rewrite it.
- Prefer short clauses separated by full stops over long clauses joined by commas or em dashes.
- Never describe what the viewer can already see on screen — explain why it matters or what it replaces.
- Do not define a term before showing what it does — show it, then name it.
- Do not write passive voice: "it was discovered that" → "they found."

---

## Formatting

- **UK English** spellings throughout (organise, optimise, colour, favourite, analyse, realise).
- **No em dashes. Ever.** Replace with commas, full stops, or rewrite the sentence. Sentences separated by full stops flow better spoken than sentences joined by dashes.

---

## Tone summary

Conversational, direct, personal. Explains concepts by showing what they replace, not by defining them abstractly. Comfortable with rhetorical questions. Never formal, never corporate, never academic. Mits sounds like a knowledgeable mate who happens to have built things and wants to show you how.
